PatientKeeper Retorno helps a doctor keep the next step visible when a patient follow-up remains unfinished. Created by Athos from Figueira, this prototype grew from the scattered contacts and incomplete follow-ups we see while supporting medical practices. All cases in this demonstration are fictional.

The doctor workspace brings the journey, consultation and contact history together. Here, a fictional bunion and bunionette surgery labels the scenario. The doctor chooses the phase, the responsible person, the next administrative action and a check date. The system does not invent surgical instructions or clinical deadlines. The practice receives an organized record without requiring reception staff to operate this demonstration.

The local version really transcribes a fictional audio file on this computer. Portuguese and English audio are supported independently from the navigation language. The doctor reviews the original transcript and selects literal segments to prepare a note with source references. This draft is an extraction, not a model-generated clinical summary or a signed medical record. Selecting a different file requires a new declaration. Changing language or tabs preserves the selected audio.

The contact routine checks the recorded preference and requires review of the exact administrative source and message. The action shown here is a simulation: no WhatsApp message is sent. A response and the next step remain visible. A refusal blocks further contact. Changing the source invalidates the earlier review. A human records the administrative resolution, so preparing a message alone cannot close the episode.

We also executed five distinct administrative queries through the AMD Token Factory service. The official usage history maps the requested Gemma model to the returned identifier. Three responses produced candidates supported by literal citations. One response abstained when the essential source was absent, and one request timed out. The final positive case ran through the product API. These experiments used the follow-up queue, not the new consultation note. The public panel displays recorded evidence matched to the current question and sources; it does not start live inference.

A clinical question, conflicting records, or changed context blocks reuse of an old answer. The original queue, fictional doctor calendar, English, Portuguese and Spanish navigation, and light and dark appearance remain available. Gemma runs on the AMD service. Audio transcription runs locally on the CPU. The public static version cannot transcribe audio or send messages. This separation makes the limits visible instead of presenting a prepared action as an executed one.

The intended value is fewer unfinished follow-ups and less repeated administrative work. We have tested the software and the synthetic workflow; operational savings and clinical outcomes have not been measured. The administrative scaffold existed before the event and is declared. Source review, real AMD experiments, and the new doctor workspace were added during the event. The public demo and repository let judges inspect the prototype, the source, and the recorded evidence.
