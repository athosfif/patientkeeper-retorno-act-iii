Hi, I’m Athos, from Figueira.

PatientKeeper Retorno grew from problems I see while working with medical practices: scattered conversations, unanswered messages, and follow-ups that never reach a clear conclusion.

A canceled appointment should not become a forgotten patient.

Our goal is to help the doctor understand what remains open, who is responsible, and which record supports the next step.

Everything in this demonstration uses fictional data. The system organizes the work, while the doctor remains responsible for decisions.

The doctor workspace brings the patient journey, consultation material, and contact history together.

Our fictional example follows a bunion and bunionette surgery episode. The doctor records the phase, responsibility, next administrative action, and a date to check progress.

The system does not invent surgical instructions or decide clinical timing.

Search, a fictional calendar, three interface languages, and light or dark appearance help make navigation straightforward.

The workflow centers on the doctor, while giving reception staff a clearer handoff and fewer scattered instructions.

In the configured local version, a short fictional audio file can be transcribed on the computer.

The doctor reviews the transcript and selects literal passages to prepare a source-linked draft. This is an extract for review, not an automatically generated diagnosis or a signed medical record.

The contact workflow checks the recorded preference and requires review of the exact source and message.

WhatsApp contact is simulated. No message is sent.

A refusal blocks contact, and changing the source invalidates the previous review. Preparing a message does not mean the follow-up is complete.

We tested Gemma through AMD Token Factory and connected PatientKeeper to an Evolus workflow using that configured Gemma connection.

One real application-to-API execution returned an administrative candidate in about eight seconds, with two exact source citations and mandatory human review.

The administrative state remained unchanged.

The application checks the current context and blocks outdated results. The doctor can inspect the original sources before deciding what to do.

This was a small synthetic pilot. The public demonstration displays recorded evidence, without live inference or audio transcription.

What could this mean for a doctor’s day?

The routine reported to us involves six records per day, with fifteen minutes of documentation each. That represents ninety minutes of daily documentation.

If an assisted workflow reduced each record to ten minutes, including preparation, review, and corrections, the difference would be thirty minutes a day.

That is an illustrative scenario, not a measured PatientKeeper result.

Our next step is to test that assumption in a controlled practice pilot.

The administrative scaffold predates the hackathon. Source review, AMD experiments, and the doctor workspace were added during the event.
